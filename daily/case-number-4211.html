Case Number 4211

September 26, 2026 · 2271 words

Vaughn Mbeki's fingers flew across the stenotype machine, capturing the proceedings of Case Number 4211 with precision. The rehabilitation facility's converted chapel was a far cry from the sterile courthouses he was accustomed to, but its acoustics were surprisingly effective for recording testimony. He had been brought in as an independent transcriptionist to document the case files of patients being discharged from the facility's program.

As he worked, Vaughn's gaze drifted towards the duty roster on the wall, where a list of staff members was scribbled in faint marker. The handwriting on one entry in particular caught his eye: "Morrow, J...". He quickly looked away, refocusing on the testimony unfolding before him. This is a normal day.

The chaplain's soothing voice filled the room as he led the patient through a series of questions about their progress. Vaughn's eyes scanned the transcript display above his machine, making sure to capture every nuance of the conversation.

As the session drew to a close, the patient's voice cracked with emotion, and the chaplain offered words of encouragement. Vaughn transcribed it all without pause: "P...please don't say that," the patient stuttered, "it's not fair."

The chaplain's expression turned sympathetic as he nodded along, his eyes locked on the patient. Vaughn's focus remained fixed on the machine, but a faint memory stirred – a conversation with a colleague about the Morrow case being particularly challenging to close.

Vaughn refocused on the task at hand, the sound of the stenotype keys clacking out a steady rhythm as he continued to transcribe the proceedings. *This is a normal day.

Vaughn's hands moved swiftly over the stenotype keys as he transcribed the chaplain's soothing words. The patient's sobs subsided, replaced by quiet sniffles. Vaughn's gaze flicked upward, meeting the chaplain's gentle smile. He nodded along with the proceedings, his focus never wavering.

As the session drew to a close, the chaplain stood to escort the patient back to their quarters. Vaughn's eyes scanned the room, taking in the neat rows of chairs and the tidy workstations. His gaze landed on the small table near the window, where he'd left his lunch. A stack of files and his steno machine sat beside a cup of cold coffee.

A faint unease crept over him as he noticed his briefcase was open, its contents spilling out onto the table. This is just a mess. His eyes darted to the neatly arranged rows of pens on the nearby desk, wondering if someone had borrowed it. He remembered now – he'd left his lunch here earlier that morning, and someone must have cleared the space for the next session.

Vaughn's gaze landed on the small stack of books on his table, their spines facing inward. His eyes widened slightly as he realized they were arranged incorrectly – his Bible was nestled between two novels, its cover askew. Must be someone playing a prank. He remembered now: he'd left them on the table after lunch, and...and maybe Emma had moved them? Yes, that's it. Emma, the administrative assistant, must have tidied up while he was in session.

Vaughn's hands continued to transcribe, his mind dismissing the minor disturbance as just another occupational hazard of working at a rehabilitation facility. He returned his focus to the stenotype machine, the rhythmic clacking of keys providing a soothing background hum.

The air in the room grew warmer, carrying the faint scent of wet wool drying too close to a fire.

Vaughn's fingers danced across the stenotype keys, the familiar rhythm of transcription lulling him into a meditative state. The proceeding continued uninterrupted, the voices of Dr. Patel and her patient weaving in and out of his awareness. He barely registered the faint scratch of Emma's pen on paper as she worked at the adjacent desk.

A slight aberration caught his attention: the photograph tacked to the wall opposite him showed a room identical to this one, but with a different configuration of chairs and tables. Vaughn's gaze flickered back to the real room – same worn linoleum, same faded cross above the door. He shifted in his seat, thinking it was just his mind playing tricks on him.

The image on the wall, however, remained stubbornly unchanged. It depicted a scene where a pair of visitors sat with Dr. Patel's patient, engaged in a warm smile-filled conversation. Vaughn distinctly remembered those chairs being positioned differently when he arrived for work that morning. His gaze darted between the photograph and reality, but the discrepancy refused to resolve itself.

Shrugging off the anomaly as a minor administrative error, Vaughn returned his focus to transcription. The words flowed smoothly beneath his fingers, but his peripheral vision picked up on another mismatch: the clock on the wall showed 9:47 AM, while the calendar on Emma's desk displayed tomorrow's date.

Vaughn's fingers paused over the keys as he gazed at Emma's desk, his mind still trying to reconcile the mismatched dates on the calendar and clock. He pushed his chair back from the stenotype machine and stood up, intending to call the facility's main number to clarify the discrepancy. But as he reached for the phone, his hand brushed against the cold metal of the wall, and he felt a sudden jolt of unease.

The phone, of course, was not where he expected it to be. He had used that wall-mounted unit countless times before; it was always there, waiting for him to call Dr. Patel or someone from security if need be. Now, however, the phone was nowhere to be seen. Vaughn's eyes scanned the console on Emma's desk again, but the phone's absence wasn't just a matter of misplaced equipment – the entire console was missing.

"What's going on?" Vaughn muttered to himself, his voice rising in pitch as panic began to seep into his chest. He spun around, taking in the unfamiliar layout of the room. The chairs were still there, but the seating arrangement had changed again. And Dr. Patel's patient – Emma's patient, now – was sitting in one of them, staring at Vaughn with an unreadable expression.

Vaughn's mind racing, he strode over to the console on the adjacent wall and lifted the receiver off its cradle. But when he dialed his own extension from memory, the phone let out a long, electronic whine before spitting out a pre-recorded message: "This line is currently unavailable due to maintenance."

"Unavailability due to maintenance?" Vaughn repeated aloud, incredulity creeping into his tone. He tried to redial, but the result was the same – an unforgiving silence on the other end of the line.

For a moment, Vaughn stood there, frozen in confusion and growing unease. Then, with a jolt of practicality, he remembered that the maintenance records would be kept somewhere in the facility's central administration office.

Vaughn's footsteps carried him down the deserted hallway, the worn soles of his shoes making soft slapping sounds on the polished linoleum. He had given up trying to contact Emma in administration – what was he going to say, anyway? He'd only have to go back and forth with someone who would just as likely tell him the line was still down.

He stopped in front of a door that had been locked before, but now swung open with an faint creak. Inside, the room seemed identical to the others – beige walls, steel table, two chairs, but...Vaughn's eyes widened as he took in the details. The chair nearest him was positioned at a slightly different angle than usual, and on its seat lay a pair of worn leather boots, polished so that they shone like dark brown honey. They looked new, but Vaughn knew better – those were his spare boots, already broken in from months of wearing them around the city.

A faint smell wafted up from the boots, something that made his stomach turn: wet wool drying too close to a fire again, that old reek of sweat and singe. Vaughn's eyes went to the floor, where he could swear he saw a faint smudge of soot, an inch off the edge of the chair leg. No one had been here since the last time...Vaughn's mind stuttered to a stop as he stared at those boots.

He took a step closer, reaching out to touch the smooth leather. That was when he noticed it: a hair caught in the stitching, an impossible shade of blonde that didn't belong to anyone he knew. *This is wrong.

Vaughn's hand closed around the chair leg, fingers tightening as if to anchor himself to reality. He raised his eyes to the door, where Patel stood frozen, her mouth a thin line. Emma's gaze darted between them, then back to Vaughn, her brow furrowed in concern.

The air in the room had changed. It wasn't just the smell of wet wool and sweat anymore; there was something else, acrid and sharp. Vaughn's nostrils flared as he breathed it in – copper, like metal burning.

He felt a subtle shift in the floorboards beneath his feet. The chair leg wobbled, then steadied. Patel took a step forward, her hand extended, but Vaughn shook his head. Don't touch it.

The temperature dropped. A faint draft caressed Vaughn's skin, carrying with it a hint of damp earth and something decaying. He felt the pressure in the room build, like a low-pitched thrumming that vibrated through every cell.

Vaughn's own breathing quickened as he stared at Patel, his heart racing to keep pace. Emma's eyes were wide, her pupils dilating as if trying to take in everything at once. The sound of their breathing filled the room – Patel's steady inhale-exhale, Vaughn's rapid cadence, and Emma's short, gasping breaths.

Something was moving, just out of sight. The floorboards creaked, a single, deliberate groan that seemed to synchronize with Vaughn's own heartbeat. The air coalesced around him, thickening into an almost palpable presence.

Vaughn's vision blurred as Patel leaned forward, his eyes locked on some invisible point beyond Vaughn's shoulder. Emma's gaze followed, her face a mask of confusion. The room's thrumming grew louder, a low-pitched whine that seemed to emanate from the very core of the steel table.

And then, in an instant, it was over. Patel's eyes snapped back into focus, his expression neutral. Vaughn's own vision cleared, but the image stuck: Patel's gaze had lingered on something behind Vaughn, something that should not be there. The sound of their breathing still filled the room, but now it seemed...off.

Vaughn's mind reeled as he stared at Patel, trying to recall the events leading up to this moment. He remembered signing the consent form, agreeing to participate in this mysterious proceeding. But what had happened since then? Patel's steady gaze was unnerving; Emma's eyes darted back and forth, searching for something.

Vaughn's hand instinctively went to his chest, where a faint scar still lingered from that fateful day. The accident on 5th Street, the one where he'd been struck by the old sedan. The pain, the paramedics, the hospital stay...it all came flooding back. And then, the silence.

A chill crept up Vaughn's spine as Patel leaned back in his chair, a faint smile playing on his lips. "You've been here before, Mr. Mbeki," he said, his voice low and detached.

Vaughn's eyes snapped to Emma, but she was staring at him, her expression a mix of confusion and horror. He felt the floor beneath him shift, like the very foundations of reality were crumbling. It couldn't be. He remembered...he remembered everything.

Patel's words hung in the air like a challenge: "You've been here before." Vaughn's mind reeled as he stared at his own hands, flexing his fingers in a futile attempt to grasp what was happening.

Vaughn's mind was numb, his thoughts disjointed and incomplete. He couldn't shake the feeling of being watched, but when he turned to glance at Emma, she was gone. Patel still sat in the chair, his smile now a thin line on his face.

The days passed in a blur, with Vaughn unable to sleep or eat. The rehabilitation facility went about its routine, unaware of the events that had transpired. Vaughn's transcripts were filed away, marked as "confidential" and "sealed". Patel's name appeared only once, in a notation on the file: "Facilitator for Special Proceeding".

Vaughn's name was crossed out of the daily log, his absence unnoticed by anyone. The facility's records showed him present for every session, every meal, every therapy group. But he wasn't there.

The morning sun streamed through the windows, casting a faint glow on the steel table where Vaughn had once sat. Emma's replacement, a new intern named Patel, was busy transcribing yesterday's proceedings. She worked quietly, her fingers flying across the keys as she typed out the transcripts in neat, precise rows.

Vaughn's mind still reeled, unable to grasp what had happened. He remembered fragments of his past, disjointed images and sounds that refused to coalesce into a coherent whole. The smell of wet wool drying too close to a fire hung heavy in the air, a constant reminder of something he couldn't quite put his finger on.

The intern paused, her eyes flicking to the clock on the wall: 9:47 AM. She typed out a notation, marking the start of another session. Vaughn's chair was empty, but the table remained, its surface scratched and worn from years of use. The smell of wet wool lingered, carried on the faint scent of smoke that clung to the air.

The intern returned to her typing, the silence broken only by the soft hum of the machines and the scratch of her fingers on the keys.
