Seed Vault Secrecy

September 21, 2026 · 2899 words

Cyrus Wren scanned the row of metal shelves, his eyes squinting against the fluorescent glare overhead. He'd been to the seed vault three times before, but this was only his second quarterly inventory on paid duty. The first time, he'd just tagged along with the head curator, Dr. Patel, who'd shown him the ropes and assigned him a few tasks to keep him out of trouble. This time, he was officially part of the team, tasked with reviewing the seed counts in Sector 3.

He checked the clipboard against the numbers on the shelf labels, making sure everything matched up. The seeds themselves were mostly dormant, stored at near-freezing temperatures and high humidity. It wasn't exactly thrilling work, but it was a chance to get out of his apartment, clear his head, and focus on something besides his own problems for a while.

As he worked, the radio on the counter above the shelves picked up a faint whisper of static. It was tuned to 93.7 FM, a frequency that usually carried the morning news from Phoenix. He'd set it to that station months ago, but nowadays all he ever got was static. He didn't bother changing the dial; it was just background noise.

Cyrus moved on to the next shelf, checking the labels and making some notes on his clipboard. The seeds were arranged by species and crop type, with each row labeled in alphabetical order. It was a system designed for efficiency, not for curiosity or wonder. He found himself running over the same rows again, double-checking the numbers just to make sure he hadn't missed anything.

The vault itself felt like a giant metal box, carved out of the mountain's solid core. The walls were made of reinforced concrete, with steel-reinforced doors and secure locks. It was a fortress for seeds, not people. Cyrus knew all this, but still, every so often, he caught himself looking around for some sign of... something else. A hidden entrance, maybe, or an alternate path that didn't exist on the maps.

He shook his head, chuckling at his own imagination. This was just a seed vault, after all.

Cyrus rounded the corner, his eyes scanning the rows of seed pods for any sign of discrepancy. Patel's team was already on their way out, having finished their section of the vault. Cyrus's gaze landed on a specific shelf, where a row of packets was stacked haphazardly.

This is what happens when Patel gets in a rush, he thought, no attention to detail.

He approached the shelf, his footsteps echoing off the concrete walls. That's when he heard it – a heartbeat. Not loud, but distinct. Cyrus's head cocked to one side as he tried to pinpoint its location.

At first, he thought it was just his imagination, but then he noticed that the sound seemed to be coming from behind the wall. He walked over to it and pressed his ear against the concrete, feeling a strange sense of trepidation.

This is ridiculous, he told himself. It's just some kind of anomaly in the ventilation system or the pipes.

He straightened up, a rational explanation already forming in his mind. Probably just a pump or a compressor kicking on at irregular intervals. He made a mental note to check with Patel about it later.

The sound stopped as abruptly as it had started. Cyrus let out a small sigh of relief, chiding himself for letting his imagination get the better of him. He returned to his task, reshelving packets and making notes on his clipboard.

As he worked, the sound didn't come back. Cyrus told himself that was a good sign – just his brain playing tricks on him. But as he turned to head down one of the corridors, he noticed something else odd: a section of the wall seemed... slightly askew.

He pushed it with his hand, and to his surprise, the entire stretch of wall creaked outward, revealing a small service tunnel beyond. Cyrus's heart skipped a beat.

That's probably just maintenance access, he thought, but a flicker of unease danced in the back of his mind.

The sound was gone now, replaced by an wrong stillness. He stepped back into the main corridor, feeling a sense of disquiet that he couldn't shake. That's when he noticed it: the escalator at the far end of the vault had stopped moving. It was frozen, one step forward and two steps back.

Cyrus approached it cautiously, his eyes scanning the surrounding area for any signs of what might have caused it to stall. But there were none – just an faint silence that seemed to swallow up every sound in the vault.

Cyrus continued the quarterly inventory, methodically recording each canister's contents and noting any discrepancies. Patel had explained the anomaly at the south wing's west storage section away as a minor miscalculation on her part, but Cyrus still felt a lingering sense of unease.

As he checked the northwest corner's east row, his mind began to wander to Patel's words. Just a misplaced decimal point , he thought, replaying the conversation in his head. Nothing to worry about. The seed vault's sterile atmosphere and faint silence seemed to seep into his thoughts like a damp shroud.

He moved on to the north wing's east storage section, his eyes scanning the rows of numbered canisters for any signs of anomalies. But as he reached the fourth row down, a face momentarily flashed in his mind – Patel's, but it was...off. The features were there, but they seemed to blur and shift like ripples on water.

Cyrus blinked, focusing on the task at hand. Must be the lack of sleep , he told himself, but the image lingered for a fraction of a second longer than he'd have liked. He shook his head, attributing it to fatigue.

He moved on, working his way through the rows with practiced efficiency. But as he entered the east storage section's west wing, another face materialized in his mind – one of his students, Emily Wilson, but her features seemed...wrong. The curve of her nose was slightly off, and her eyes seemed to be looking in two different directions at once.

Cyrus's brow furrowed as he tried to recall the specifics of her face. It's just a trick of the light , he told himself, but the memory refused to cohere. He shook his head again, continuing the inventory with a growing sense of disquiet.

A faint hum of fluorescent lights underscored the silence, and Cyrus felt his unease deepen. He tried to push it aside, focusing on the task at hand, but the faces continued to flicker in his mind like candle flames in a draft – Patel's, Emily's, and now one more: his own reflection in the windowpane of the control room, but his eyes seemed...wrong.

Cyrus pushed through the doors of the control room, into the main vault's central hub. He spotted a bank of payphones on one wall, their old-fashioned receivers cradled in worn leather straps. He approached them with a growing sense of desperation, fumbling for his phone card. The thought of Emily's calm voice was suddenly crucial to him – if he could just confirm that everything was okay outside...

But as he inserted the card and punched in the numbers, the payphones' displays blinked stubbornly to life: Service Unavailable. Cyrus frowned, tugging on the cord to ensure it wasn't simply a dead connection. That's impossible, he thought. The vault had backup lines for emergencies – this was a standard procedure they'd drilled into the quarterly inventory teams.

He tried another phone, and another, but each time the same message flashed on the display. Cyrus's frustration mounted as he paced across the hub, his footsteps echoing off the cold stone walls. He paused in front of a computer terminal, typing in his login credentials – but even this basic request was denied: Network Unavailable. A growing unease crept up his spine as he checked the backup power sources, the backup generators, everything that should be working...

A faint tremble had begun to run through his hands. Cyrus pushed it aside, telling himself it was just fatigue or worry – but something about this failure felt different from the earlier glitches. The lights in the vault seemed to flicker slightly, and for a moment, he thought he heard a distant hum, almost inaudible. He pressed on the control room door, trying to shake off the feeling of being trapped. Just get out of here, he told himself, but the doors slid shut behind him with an ominous silence.

Cyrus stood frozen at the control room door, his hand still raised from pressing the button that had sealed him in. The faint hum he thought he'd heard earlier seemed to have grown louder, a low thrumming that vibrated through the soles of his shoes.

He shook his head, trying to clear it. Fatigue, worry – anything but this creeping sense of unease. But as he turned away from the door, he found himself struggling to recall Emily's face. The features were there, but they kept shifting, like sand between his fingers. He concentrated harder, focus on her smile, but it was gone, lost in a fog of... something.

"Cyrus?" he whispered, feeling a jolt of panic as the name slipped from his mind. "No, no, Emily's face... I remember Emily."

But as he walked away from the control room, he caught himself staring at Patel's face on the ID card display, and his own eyes felt like they were trying to hold onto something that refused to be grasped.

The seed vault's rows of shelves stretched out before him, a seemingly endless array of containers storing the future. Cyrus's gaze fell on a nearby console, where a list of inventory checks seemed to blur together on the screen. He leaned forward, squinting at the numbers... but they all looked the same.

He took a step back, his eyes widening as he realized he couldn't quite hold onto the memory of Patel's voice either – or her words from their last conversation just hours ago. It was like trying to grasp water, only to have it slip through his fingers.

The air conditioning hummed on, its gentle thrumming now making him feel queasy. Cyrus looked around, disoriented, as if he'd stumbled into a place that wasn't quite his own. The stopped escalator outside the control room seemed to have shifted another step down, its metal surface creaking softly under some unseen weight.

He approached it cautiously, feeling the chill of the concrete beneath his feet. As he passed through, the stillness enveloped him like a shroud. He paused at his own locker, where his folded blanket lay neat and tidy on top of his worn backpack. The edge of the blanket was folded just so – the way he always did it.

But something was off about the symmetry. Something... wrong.

Cyrus's eyes lingered on the blanket's folded edge. Something wrong. He couldn't quite put his finger on it. As he turned to rejoin the inventory team, a faint hum began to build in the silence. It started as a low thrum, almost imperceptible, but grew louder with each passing second.

He stopped, his ears straining to pinpoint the source of the sound. It seemed to be emanating from the control room itself, or rather, from within its walls. The hum began to take on a rhythmic quality, pulsing in time with his own heartbeat.

Cyrus's skin prickled as he felt the temperature in the room drop by a few degrees. He could sense it, even though he wasn't sure how – a subtle shift in air pressure that made his breathing quicken. His chest compressed and expanded against the confines of his jacket.

He tried to take a step back, but his feet felt rooted to the spot. The hum grew louder still, until it was almost a low roar. Cyrus's eyes darted towards the control room door, which now hung slightly ajar. A faint scent wafted out – dry earth and decaying leaves, like the air after a long drought.

The sound of his own breathing filled his ears, harsh and labored. He felt suffocated, trapped in a space that seemed to be shrinking by the second. The blanket's folded edge receded from his mind as panic set in. He had to move. Had to get out. But something – or someone – was holding him in place.

Cyrus's gaze snapped back to the control room door, now creaking open on its hinges with a slow, deliberate motion. The darkness within seemed to be pressing forward, like a living entity. He felt his breath catch in his throat as the sound of the hum reached a deafening crescendo.

The control room door swung open, its hinges shrieking in protest as if rebelling against the movement. Cyrus's gaze was fixed on the dark shape emerging from within. Patel stood at the threshold, her eyes empty of any concern or alarm. "Cyrus, it's time," she said, her voice low and even.

A chill ran up his spine as he realized that this was not Patel from the quarterly inventory meetings. Not the one who had been explaining crop rotation strategies or warning about fungal outbreaks. This Patel was...different.

He tried to take a step back, but something held him in place – the same presence that had been creeping over him all day. The blanketed air seemed to vibrate with an almost imperceptible weight, making every movement feel like a betrayal of some fundamental rule.

"Cyrus, please," Patel said again, her voice soothing and detached. "Come along. It's time for...evaluation."

He remembered the phone calls – so many phone calls – and the calm voice on the other end. "I'll be right there," it would say, or "Excellent work, Cyrus." The voice had been coaching him through his teaching assignments, through the school's crisis management protocols. He recalled trying to call his sister, Emily, but getting only a recorded message. And then he remembered the quarterly inventory reports – the discrepancies in storage levels that had been written off as minor anomalies.

A jolt of comprehension ran through him like electricity: the voice on the phone was not just any coach or administrator. It was the one who had been guiding this operation from the start – the one who had designed this seed vault and the inventory system within it. The one who had manipulated him into participating in the quarterly evaluation, all under the guise of routine maintenance.

Patel's hand closed around his arm, and he felt a shiver run down his spine as she led him forward, toward some destination that now seemed to be predetermined rather than random. "Let's go, Cyrus," she said, her voice still calm, still detached.

The days blended together as Patel kept him within the vault's confines. Cyrus lost count of the hours, the days, the weeks. Outside, the sun rose and set without his knowledge, and the seasons came and went like whispers on a distant wind. The seed vault remained locked in its frozen state, the inventory system continuing to log each item with precision.

A new team arrived for their quarterly rotation, but Cyrus was not introduced as one of them. Patel simply led him through the routines, her presence a constant reminder that he was no longer in control. His mind wandered, trying to grasp the full extent of what had transpired. Was it some sort of experiment? Had they been studying human behavior under duress? He pushed these questions aside, unable to focus on anything beyond his immediate reality.

The new team members went about their duties, performing tasks with a detached efficiency that chilled Cyrus. Patel explained procedures to them as if he were not there, her voice steady and flat. She kept him at arm's length, feeding him ration packs and monitoring his physical condition.

As the inventory process continued, Cyrus found himself watching the new team members work in silence, their movements choreographed with a precision that unnerved him. He tried to remember what day it was, but the absence of windows or any clear indication of time had disoriented him completely.

A piece of paper fluttered from his jacket pocket and floated to the floor. It was an old calendar page from a month prior to his leave of absence. The creased surface and faded ink told their own story – that, like the world outside, Cyrus's past was slowly becoming irrelevant. The seed vault had become his new reality.

The air conditioning unit in the corner hummed on, its motor whirring with an almost human cadence. Patel moved to stand beside it, her eyes fixed on the log screen. Cyrus watched as she checked the schedule for his next ration pack delivery and nodded to herself, a small gesture of approval.

An old rusted sedan caked in grey silt sat outside the vault's entrance, idling silently. The tires were flat, the engine dead, and no one had bothered to remove it. It stood like a fossil from another era, its presence a testament to the indifference of time passing on without him. In the background, the stopped escalator groaned under the weight of footsteps, its steps stuttering once, twice, then settling back into stillness.
